How these numbers work

Where the game-i (JP ¥) and Sensor Tower (combined $) figures come from — and why they change.

This site doesn't measure or estimate anything itself. It re-presents two third-party datasets in a more readable form: game-i.daa.jp's per-banner Japan revenue (the default, in ¥), and the community Sensor Tower monthly reports' combined worldwide revenue (the “Sensor Tower $” toggle, in $). They measure different things and are shown side by side — never added together.

What the number is

An estimate of how much a banner earned from mobile gacha in Japan, shown here in yen. game-i reports it in “G” — a deliberately vague unit it says just means “about” (〜ぐらい). By common convention 1億G ≈ ¥100 million (so 1G ≈ ¥1), which is how the yen figures here are derived.

Where it comes from

game-i records each app's App Store / Google Play top-grossing rank every day at midnight (JST), converts that daily rank into an estimated daily revenue using a model calibrated against companies' publicly disclosed earnings, and adds up the days a banner runs. It's a rank → revenue estimate, not reported sales.

Banner A peaked at #2, banner B at #3 — so why does game-i say B earned more?

Because a rank isn't worth a fixed amount. game-i multiplies each day's rank by a daily value that changes with the date (its own note: 加算値は月／日によって変動します — “the value varies by month and day”). Its live table shows the same rank paying different amounts day to day — e.g. #2 was worth ≈¥344M on Jul 26 but ≈¥282M on Jul 27.

The biggest swing is the start of the month: game-i deliberately sets early-month days as the highest of the month — 月初の加算値は月内で最も高い数値 — because credit-card billing and carrier spending-limit resets push spending up then (it cites New Year's Day sales records). So whichever banner's peak lands nearer the 1st gets a bigger multiplier. A #3 that peaked on the 2nd can out-earn a #2 that peaked mid-month — even though #2 is the better rank on any single day. (There's also a smaller correction based on how spread-out that day's rankings were — 標準偏差に応じて補正.)

In short: rank sets the shape, the calendar scales it. That's why the cumulative total can rank a lower-peaking banner higher, and why the daily ranks are the more dependable part of game-i's data than the exact yen.

How a day's revenue is split between banners

These are game-i's own stated rules, from its ガチャ売上分析について page. The model assumes all of the app's estimated revenue that day is gacha revenue (推定売上が全てガチャによるものと仮定), and assigns it to whatever banners are running:

  • Banners running at the same time split the day equally — 同時に複数のガチャが開催されていた場合は売上を等分します.
  • A banner's first day is weighted up 80%, its last day down 80% (launch rush vs. wind-down) — ガチャ期間初日は分配率を80%アップ／最終日は80%ダウン.
  • Busy periods (New Year, Golden Week) run many banners at once, so each individual banner can look lower than it really earned.
  • Rank history only exists from 2018, so nothing older can be counted.

The published figures bear this out: adding up every banner in a year comes to 88–98% of game-i's own monthly totals, never more (the gap is days no banner was listed), and two banners on exactly the same dates carry exactly equal figures — which is what an equal split produces. game-i's own summary of its precision: どこまでいってもどんぶり勘定です — “it's rough accounting however you slice it.”

On the charts, a hatched section of a bar (and a “▨ shared” tag) marks the portion of a run that overlapped another listed banner — i.e. revenue that was split rather than earned alone. This shows up mostly on games that run several banners at once (like Umamusume); HoYo titles usually merge simultaneous characters into one “A&B” banner, so the split happens inside a single entry and the bar stays solid.

Does it change after day 1?

Yes — but mostly in the first few days. Each day the banner is live adds that day's estimate from the game's current top-grossing rank. Because a banner spikes to its highest rank at launch and then steadily falls, most of the increase lands in the opening days; after that the daily additions get small and the total barely moves. It stops changing once the game drops out of the tracked ranking (below ~rank 200) or the banner ends.

What's covered — and what isn't

Japan only, iOS (App Store, incl. iPad) + Android (Google Play). It excludes overseas stores, PC and browser versions (DMM / mobage / GREE), and merchandise — so a game's true worldwide revenue is meaningfully higher than what you see here.

How accurate is it?

Directional, not exact. game-i itself calls the method どんぶり勘定 — a deliberate back-of-the-envelope built from statistics, regression analysis and machine prediction, with no guarantee of matching official figures. Known blind spots it discloses:

  • Days below ~rank 200 can't be tracked and are counted as ¥0 — weak banners get underestimated.
  • An app holding #1 can earn far beyond the model's ceiling (青天井), so monster banners get underestimated too.
  • The model is regressed from iOS ranks — if a game's Android rank diverges a lot, the estimate skews.
  • iOS-only apps should be read at about half the shown value; stores like Amazon/mobage aren't included at all.

It's better for top-ranked titles (more disclosed earnings to calibrate against) and rougher for smaller ones. Use these numbers to compare banners, not as official figures.

The “JP store rank today” tile

Each game's tiles include its current top-grossing rank (iOS & Android, Japan) straight from game-i, with a two-month sparkline of the daily iOS rank — the raw ingredient every estimate on this page is cooked from. Gaps in the line are days below the trackable ~top 200.

Reading a banner's daily rank curve

Click any banner to see its daily iOS top-grossing rank across the whole run (#1 = top of the chart). A few things to keep in mind:

  • Rank is snapshotted once, at midnight JST — so a banner's launch day can read below-200 if it went live after that snapshot; the spike shows up the next day.
  • Breaks in the line are days the app sat below the trackable ~top 200 — game-i can't see those and counts them as ¥0.
  • It's iOS only: game-i keeps a daily history for the App Store but only a live snapshot for Google Play, so there's no daily Android curve.
  • For a banner still running, the curve stops at today and the figures shown are the running totals so far, not the final ones.

The banner card also shows an estimated revenue build-up. game-i reports only one total per banner, so this splits that total across the run by each day's rank (using game-i's published rank→revenue curve) purely to illustrate how the total accumulated. The per-day yen is a reconstruction — the run's total still equals game-i's own figure — not a separately reported number.

What this layer is

game-i only covers Japan. The “Sensor Tower $” toggle switches every figure on the page — the bars, the ranking order, the Graph, and the header tiles (Total revenue, Highest banner, Highest month, Average) — to a game's estimated combined worldwide revenue in US dollars, and a short disclaimer appears above the chart. The two layers measure different things, so the ¥ and $ are shown side by side and are never added together.

Where the numbers come from

The monthly “Gacha Revenue / Sensor Tower” reports posted to r/gachagaming, read straight from the report images. Coverage runs from October 2021 to the latest published month; older, region-summed months are marked with a * and are approximate.

What each figure means

A game's combined worldwide mobile revenue for a whole month — every regional server summed (JP + global/US + KR where they exist) plus China. It is not per-banner and not Japan-only, so it isn't directly comparable to game-i's ¥ figure.

The China adjustment — the 1.75× rule

Sensor Tower's “global” line historically left China out, and where China was reported it was often iOS only. In those cases China Android is modelled at 1.75× the China-iOS figure — the iOS-to-Android ratio seen in the recent reports that split both — so a month's China total is:

China total = China iOS + (China iOS × 1.75) = China iOS × 2.75

A few of the earliest months had no China figure at all; those are tagged global only and are undercounted. From 2024 onward the reports break China out cleanly, so no reconstruction is needed.

Where the China number came from — 2022 vs 2023

The method above is the same throughout; what changed year to year is where the China-iOS number was found.

2022 & early 2023 — from the report's own China row. The community “sensortower stats” spreadsheet listed each region on its own line, including an explicit CN row (iOS only, Android blank). So the month is just the global/US total plus that China row, grossed up for Android:

Example — Genshin Impact · January 2022
global (US) $102M  ·  China iOS $43M
China = $43M × 2.75 = $118.25M ($43M iOS + $75.25M modelled Android)
combined = $102M + $118.25M = $220.25M

Mid-to-late 2023 — from the post's comments. Those reports shared only the single global figure, not China. The China-iOS number was taken from the post's comments — fairly upvoted, but less reliable. It's kept because, without it, 2023 would read oddly low next to the years on either side:

Example — Honkai: Star Rail · August 2023
global $49M  ·  China iOS (from comments) $32M
China = $32M × 2.75 = $88M ($32M iOS + $56M modelled Android)
combined = $49M + $88M = $137M

2024 onward — read straight from the image. From 2024 the monthly reports already publish each game as a single combined figure with China (both platforms) folded in — the 1.75× Android split is baked into the number the report shows. So these months need no comment-digging and no reconstruction on our side: the value is simply read off the report image as-is.

Per-banner is assumed, not reported

Sensor Tower never breaks a month down by banner. This site credits a banner with the same share of the month's combined total as it earned of game-i's JP revenue that month, then sums those slices across every month the banner ran (a banner spanning two months adds both). Click any banner for the full month-by-month math, who else ran each month, and whether its run is only partly covered.

Why it's rough

  • It inherits game-i's JP-share estimate and assumes JP spending mirrors worldwide spending for that banner.
  • The China ×1.75 Android figure is a model, not a measurement — and some 2023 China numbers came from comments.
  • * = an approximate, region-summed month; global only = no China figure that month; partial = the banner also ran in months not yet reported.

Treat the $ figures as ballpark context for scale and trend, not precise sales — and, again, never add them to the ¥ figures.

What this layer is

A third, independent estimate: 天天背锅崩坏娘's monthly 二次元手游全球总流水 ranking on Bilibili — 57 consecutive months from November 2021 to today, the longest continuous CN-side gacha revenue record that exists. Roughly 118k followers and 22.7M views across the series.

It is global revenue across every platform — mobile plus PC, PlayStation and overseas Xbox — reported in yuan. That makes it a different measurement again from game-i (Japan, mobile) and Sensor Tower (worldwide, mobile). All three are shown side by side and never added together.

How the author calculates it

Stated on-screen in every episode, under the heading 「流水计算方法」:

  • Base data inferred from Sensor Tower / App Store / Google Play rankings — a rank→revenue estimate, not measured sales.
  • China Android ≈ 2× China iOS (otome titles 1.5–2×).
  • Overseas mobile = Google Play + iOS summed.
  • PC / PlayStation / Xbox estimated per game from official disclosures, genre and platform launch dates, with per-platform coefficients between 0.3 and 1.8.

The per-game coefficients themselves are never published, so no individual figure here is independently reproducible.

Why miHoYo titles are a range

miHoYo sells top-ups through its own 支付中心 portal, bypassing the stores. Those takings have never been disclosed, so the author excludes them entirely and exempts miHoYo from the platform coefficients above. Every miHoYo card carries the on-screen note 注：暂无支付中心数据.

Genshin · Star Rail · Zenless Zone Zero are understated by an unknown amount, and published as a band rather than a point.

Example — Zenless Zone Zero · July 2026
on-screen caption: 正片：绝区零（国产，米游），7月总流水：130248万~155058万
= CN¥1.30B – CN¥1.55B (1万 = 10,000 yuan)

The metric changes basis at Nov 2023

Captions up to October 2023 read 总收入; from November 2023 they read 总流水. In Chinese industry usage 收入 is normally revenue net of the store's cut and 流水 is gross billings — roughly a 30% difference. Whether the underlying basis changed or the author only tightened his wording is unverified, so the two halves are labelled per month and a banner or period spanning the change is tagged mixed basis. Don't read across it as one line.

How the figures are read

The author publishes no table — his own note says 这不是表格视频 — so the numbers exist only as burned-in captions in the video. They're extracted by OCR (scripts/scrape_bilibili_cn.py), with each entry's figure decided by a vote across every frame it appears in, a rank-ordering check (the chart counts down, so revenue must not decrease), and a spot-check of sampled months read by eye against the video. Rows where the reading was shakier carry a lower confidence in the data file.

When a month appears — and why the newest one is missing

This layer isn't a live feed. Each month exists only once the author has edited and uploaded that month's video, and he posts on roughly the 12th to 18th of the following month. So there is always a gap of two to three weeks at the end: through the first half of any month, the previous month simply has no figure yet, and the site shows nothing rather than guessing.

A missing newest month therefore means “not published yet”, not “zero” and not “an error”. It fills itself in automatically the next time the data is refreshed after his upload.

The same waiting period applies to the Sensor Tower layer for its own reasons, so it's normal for the last month on the page to be complete in yen (game-i updates daily) while both comparison layers are still blank.

Per-banner is assumed, not reported

As with Sensor Tower, only a monthly total per game is published. A banner is credited with the same share of the CN month as it earned of game-i's Japan month, summed across the months it ran. This is a weaker assumption here than on the ST tab: game-i measures Japan mobile, while this total spans the whole world and includes PC and console, so a banner's Japanese share is a rougher proxy. Click any banner and open its CN ranking tab for the month-by-month math.

Why it's rough

  • Rank-derived estimates layered on further estimates — a Tencent News survey of these community charts put it as 在数据网站预估的基础上再次预估.
  • Per-game coefficients are unpublished; iOS is visible, Android and PC are not.
  • miHoYo figures exclude 支付中心 and are understated.
  • Figures are OCR'd from video frames, not read from a table.

Use it for scale and trend, and as a second opinion against the Sensor Tower layer — not as sales.

What this is

Not an estimate — the actual ranking Apple publishes for the China iPhone App Store, top grossing, all apps. A position, not a revenue figure. It appears on a banner's CN chart tab as the daily line for that run.

All apps is the point. Games sit in one list with Douyin, WeChat, Alipay and the video apps, so a banner launch that climbs past them is a far stronger signal than moving inside a games-only chart.

One source, every day

Every day comes from AppFollow's record of the chart, read from a signed-in account: 200 places deep, for every day since Genshin's launch on 28 September 2020 — 2,159 days in all.

It deliberately isn't mixed with anything else. Apple's own feed stops at 100, Appfigures' archived pages at 50, and each source reads the chart at its own hour — so a game "missing" from a day would mean below #10, below #50 or below #200 depending on who recorded it, and two days would not be comparable. Those other sources are still collected and cross-checked against this one; they just don't go into the line you see.

Sixteen days are absent because AppFollow never recorded a grossing chart for them: 28 Nov 2020, 22 Apr – 4 May 2023 and 8–9 Dec 2023. On those dates it holds the free and paid charts only. They show as blanks rather than being filled from a different source.

How to read the line

  • A point is the game's position that day.
  • A tick along the bottom is a day the game wasn't in the top 200 — a real reading, not missing data.
  • A blank stretch is one of those sixteen days.

Hover any day on the line for its date, rank, how deep that day's chart is known, and which source it came from.

Why a day can disagree with another source

Apple's China chart changes a handful of times a day at irregular hours, and every recorder reads it at its own moment. A day here can therefore differ by a few places from Apple's own feed or from diandian without either being wrong. Checked against diandian's record, our snapshots matched 16 of 19 days exactly across the top 20, the rest differing only in the hour they were read.

The comparison under each banner

When a banner has China days, its CN chart tab places the run against this game's other banners: where its opening rank sits within the version, the year and all-time, and how its peak and median compare with a contemporary banner — the same year, not the game's launch era, so a 2026 run isn't measured against 2020 form.

Below that sit the banners whose openings were closest to this one, with every revenue figure that exists for them. The headline figure there is game-i's Japanese estimate — the only per-banner revenue anyone publishes — so the closing line ("those five earned X to Y") is an association across two markets, not a forecast: a China rank placed next to Japanese revenue, over a handful of runs. A banner can open high on a rerun and earn little, or hold a modest rank for weeks and out-earn a spike.

The wording changes with how much is actually known: a finished run states what it earned against what similar openings earned, a run a week in says what it is tracking towards, and a run a few days old says plainly that it's too early to call. Everything in it is measured except that last step.

The forward-looking part — what a run is tracking towards — also appears on the game-i tab, since that is where the yen figure it is guessing at lives. It shows up only while a banner is still running with at least three days left: once a run is over, or within a day or two of over, game-i's own figure is all but final and an estimate of it would add nothing. The comparison itself (where the run sat, and what similar openings earned) stays on finished banners, because that is measurement rather than prediction.

What it is not

A rank is not revenue. It says a game out-earned everything below it that day, not by how much — and nothing about Android, which China's App Store has no equivalent of. For money, use the three revenue layers; for “did this banner break through”, use this.
