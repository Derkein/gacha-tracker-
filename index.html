How these numbers work

Where the game-i (JP ¥) and Sensor Tower (combined $) figures come from — and why they change.

This site doesn't measure or estimate anything itself. It re-presents two third-party datasets in a more readable form: game-i.daa.jp's per-banner Japan revenue (the default, in ¥), and the community Sensor Tower monthly reports' combined worldwide revenue (the “Sensor Tower $” toggle, in $). They measure different things and are shown side by side — never added together.

What the number is

An estimate of how much a banner earned from mobile gacha in Japan, shown here in yen. game-i reports it in “G” — a deliberately vague unit it says just means “about” (〜ぐらい). By common convention 1億G ≈ ¥100 million (so 1G ≈ ¥1), which is how the yen figures here are derived.

Where it comes from

game-i records each app's App Store / Google Play top-grossing rank every day at midnight (JST), converts that daily rank into an estimated daily revenue using a model calibrated against companies' publicly disclosed earnings, and adds up the days a banner runs. It's a rank → revenue estimate, not reported sales.

Banner A peaked at #2, banner B at #3 — so why does game-i say B earned more?

Because a rank isn't worth a fixed amount. game-i multiplies each day's rank by a daily value that changes with the date (its own note: 加算値は月／日によって変動します — “the value varies by month and day”). Its live table shows the same rank paying different amounts day to day — e.g. #2 was worth ≈¥344M on Jul 26 but ≈¥282M on Jul 27.

The biggest swing is the start of the month: game-i deliberately sets early-month days as the highest of the month — 月初の加算値は月内で最も高い数値 — because credit-card billing and carrier spending-limit resets push spending up then (it cites New Year's Day sales records). So whichever banner's peak lands nearer the 1st gets a bigger multiplier. A #3 that peaked on the 2nd can out-earn a #2 that peaked mid-month — even though #2 is the better rank on any single day. (There's also a smaller correction based on how spread-out that day's rankings were — 標準偏差に応じて補正.)

In short: rank sets the shape, the calendar scales it. That's why the cumulative total can rank a lower-peaking banner higher, and why the daily ranks are the more dependable part of game-i's data than the exact yen.

How a day's revenue is split between banners

The model assumes all of the app's estimated revenue that day is gacha revenue, and assigns it to whatever banners are running:

  • Banners running at the same time split the day equally.
  • A banner's first day is weighted up 80%, its last day down 80% (launch rush vs. wind-down).
  • Busy periods (New Year, Golden Week) run many banners at once, so each individual banner can look lower than it really earned.
  • Rank history only exists from 2018, so nothing older can be counted.

On the charts, a hatched section of a bar (and a “⇄ shared” tag) marks the portion of a run that overlapped another banner — i.e. revenue that was split. This shows up mostly on event games like FGO or Arknights; HoYo titles usually merge simultaneous characters into one “A&B” banner, so they rarely split.

Does it change after day 1?

Yes — but mostly in the first few days. Each day the banner is live adds that day's estimate from the game's current top-grossing rank. Because a banner spikes to its highest rank at launch and then steadily falls, most of the increase lands in the opening days; after that the daily additions get small and the total barely moves. It stops changing once the game drops out of the tracked ranking (below ~rank 200) or the banner ends.

What's covered — and what isn't

Japan only, iOS (App Store, incl. iPad) + Android (Google Play). It excludes overseas stores, PC and browser versions (DMM / mobage / GREE), and merchandise — so a game's true worldwide revenue is meaningfully higher than what you see here.

How accurate is it?

Directional, not exact. game-i itself calls the method どんぶり勘定 — a deliberate back-of-the-envelope built from statistics, regression analysis and machine prediction, with no guarantee of matching official figures. Known blind spots it discloses:

  • Days below ~rank 200 can't be tracked and are counted as ¥0 — weak banners get underestimated.
  • An app holding #1 can earn far beyond the model's ceiling (青天井), so monster banners get underestimated too.
  • The model is regressed from iOS ranks — if a game's Android rank diverges a lot, the estimate skews.
  • iOS-only apps should be read at about half the shown value; stores like Amazon/mobage aren't included at all.

It's better for top-ranked titles (more disclosed earnings to calibrate against) and rougher for smaller ones. Use these numbers to compare banners, not as official figures.

The “JP store rank today” tile

Each game's tiles include its current top-grossing rank (iOS & Android, Japan) straight from game-i, with a two-month sparkline of the daily iOS rank — the raw ingredient every estimate on this page is cooked from. Gaps in the line are days below the trackable ~top 200.

Reading a banner's daily rank curve

Click any banner to see its daily iOS top-grossing rank across the whole run (#1 = top of the chart). A few things to keep in mind:

  • Rank is snapshotted once, at midnight JST — so a banner's launch day can read below-200 if it went live after that snapshot; the spike shows up the next day.
  • Breaks in the line are days the app sat below the trackable ~top 200 — game-i can't see those and counts them as ¥0.
  • It's iOS only: game-i keeps a daily history for the App Store but only a live snapshot for Google Play, so there's no daily Android curve.
  • For a banner still running, the curve stops at today and the figures shown are the running totals so far, not the final ones.

The banner card also shows an estimated revenue build-up. game-i reports only one total per banner, so this splits that total across the run by each day's rank (using game-i's published rank→revenue curve) purely to illustrate how the total accumulated. The per-day yen is a reconstruction — the run's total still equals game-i's own figure — not a separately reported number.

What this layer is

game-i only covers Japan. The “Sensor Tower $” toggle switches every figure on the page — the bars, the ranking order, the Graph, and the header tiles (Total revenue, Highest banner, Highest month, Average) — to a game's estimated combined worldwide revenue in US dollars, and a short disclaimer appears above the chart. The two layers measure different things, so the ¥ and $ are shown side by side and are never added together.

Where the numbers come from

The monthly “Gacha Revenue / Sensor Tower” reports posted to r/gachagaming, read straight from the report images. Coverage runs from October 2021 to the latest published month; older, region-summed months are marked with a * and are approximate.

What each figure means

A game's combined worldwide mobile revenue for a whole month — every regional server summed (JP + global/US + KR where they exist) plus China. It is not per-banner and not Japan-only, so it isn't directly comparable to game-i's ¥ figure.

The China adjustment — the 1.75× rule

Sensor Tower's “global” line historically left China out, and where China was reported it was often iOS only. In those cases China Android is modelled at 1.75× the China-iOS figure — the iOS-to-Android ratio seen in the recent reports that split both — so a month's China total is:

China total = China iOS + (China iOS × 1.75) = China iOS × 2.75

A few of the earliest months had no China figure at all; those are tagged global only and are undercounted. From 2024 onward the reports break China out cleanly, so no reconstruction is needed.

Where the China number came from — 2022 vs 2023

The method above is the same throughout; what changed year to year is where the China-iOS number was found.

2022 & early 2023 — from the report's own China row. The community “sensortower stats” spreadsheet listed each region on its own line, including an explicit CN row (iOS only, Android blank). So the month is just the global/US total plus that China row, grossed up for Android:

Example — Genshin Impact · January 2022
global (US) $102M  ·  China iOS $43M
China = $43M × 2.75 = $118.25M ($43M iOS + $75.25M modelled Android)
combined = $102M + $118.25M = $220.25M

Mid-to-late 2023 — from the post's comments. Those reports shared only the single global figure, not China. The China-iOS number was taken from the post's comments — fairly upvoted, but less reliable. It's kept because, without it, 2023 would read oddly low next to the years on either side:

Example — Honkai: Star Rail · August 2023
global $49M  ·  China iOS (from comments) $32M
China = $32M × 2.75 = $88M ($32M iOS + $56M modelled Android)
combined = $49M + $88M = $137M

2024 onward — read straight from the image. From 2024 the monthly reports already publish each game as a single combined figure with China (both platforms) folded in — the 1.75× Android split is baked into the number the report shows. So these months need no comment-digging and no reconstruction on our side: the value is simply read off the report image as-is.

Per-banner is assumed, not reported

Sensor Tower never breaks a month down by banner. This site credits a banner with the same share of the month's combined total as it earned of game-i's JP revenue that month, then sums those slices across every month the banner ran (a banner spanning two months adds both). Click any banner for the full month-by-month math, who else ran each month, and whether its run is only partly covered.

Why it's rough

  • It inherits game-i's JP-share estimate and assumes JP spending mirrors worldwide spending for that banner.
  • The China ×1.75 Android figure is a model, not a measurement — and some 2023 China numbers came from comments.
  • * = an approximate, region-summed month; global only = no China figure that month; partial = the banner also ran in months not yet reported.

Treat the $ figures as ballpark context for scale and trend, not precise sales — and, again, never add them to the ¥ figures.
